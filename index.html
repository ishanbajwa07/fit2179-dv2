1. Australia's domestic tourism in 2025 — four quarters, one story

Across 2025, Australians took over 113 million domestic overnight trips. New South Wales and Victoria dominate in trip volume, while Queensland remains a major contributor across all quarters. Domestic travel softens through the middle of the year, reaching its lowest point in the September quarter, before rising to a December summer peak.

🔍 Key insight: Domestic overnight trips hit their lowest point in the September quarter — the heart of the Australian winter — before surging to a December peak as summer holidays begin. NSW contributes the largest share across every quarter, while Queensland shows the most stable volume, reflecting its year-round appeal.

2. Not all states share equally in tourism

New South Wales and Victoria attract the most domestic visitors in absolute terms, but Queensland punches above its weight in total visitor nights — reflecting longer average stays driven by its coastal and tropical destinations. The Northern Territory and Tasmania remain small in volume despite their strong tourist appeal.

3. Asia dominates international arrivals — but spend per visitor tells a different story

New Zealand sends the most visitors to Australia by volume, but China generates the highest spend per visitor — over $11,000 per trip compared to around $1,800 for New Zealand visitors. Circle size shows visitor volume; colour intensity shows spend per visitor, directly revealing which markets are the most valuable. The United Kingdom recorded the strongest growth between 2024 and 2025, rising 19%.

🔍 Key insight: China's visitor numbers grew 17.5% from 2024 to 2025 — the largest absolute increase of any market — while its spend per visitor remains the highest of any country, signalling that returning Chinese visitors are staying longer and spending significantly more per trip.

🔍 Key insight: The Northern Territory peaks strongly in the September quarter — its dry season — while NSW and Victoria peak in March as summer holidays wind down. Queensland is unusually consistent across all four quarters, reflecting its year-round tourism appeal.

5. More visitors does not always mean more spend

Visitor volume and spend are strongly correlated, but notable exceptions exist. Queensland generates more spend than Victoria despite recording fewer visitor nights — suggesting higher per-night expenditure driven by holiday spending. Bubble size shows the scale of international visitors to each state, adding a third dimension to the domestic picture.

6. Year on year and state by state — the detail behind the numbers

Comparing December Quarter 2024 to December Quarter 2025 reveals that most states held steady or grew slightly in visitor nights, but spending patterns diverged.
