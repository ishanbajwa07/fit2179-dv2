Australia's Tourism Story

Who visits, where they go, how much they spend — and how COVID reshaped it all

1. A decade of growth — then a cliff edge

Australian tourism grew steadily for a decade before COVID-19 wiped out international travel almost entirely in 2020. Domestic travel recovered faster, but international visitors are still catching up to 2019 levels.

🔍 Key insight: International visitor numbers collapsed by 97% in 2021, while domestic trips fell only 43% — Australians kept travelling within the country even when borders were closed.

2. Not all states share equally in tourism

New South Wales and Victoria dominate visitor numbers, but Queensland punches above its weight in visitor nights — driven by its coastal and tropical destinations. The Northern Territory and Tasmania remain relatively small in absolute terms despite their appeal.

🔍 Key insight: NSW, VIC and QLD together account for over 70% of all domestic visitor nights, reflecting their population size, infrastructure, and tourism assets.

3. Asia drives international tourism — but concentration is a risk

Australia's international visitors are heavily concentrated in a few key markets. China was the largest source before COVID, but its recovery has been slower than other markets. New Zealand remains the most consistent year-round contributor.

🔍 Key insight: China's visitor numbers in 2023 are still only 31% of 2019 levels — the slowest recovery of any top-10 market. New Zealand and the US have recovered to over 78% of pre-COVID volumes.

4. COVID's impact was sudden — recovery has been uneven

Use the timeline below to zoom into any period. The 2020 international collapse was near-total. Domestic travel bounced back in mid-2021 as state borders reopened, but spending took longer to recover as consumer confidence lagged visitor volumes.

☝ Drag on the bottom chart to zoom into a specific time period

🔍 Key insight: The reopening of international borders in February 2022 triggered an immediate spike in arrivals, but it took until late 2023 for spending levels to approach 2019 benchmarks.

5. States that attract more visitors also earn more — but not always

Visitor volume and spend are correlated, but not perfectly. Some states attract high numbers of short-stay visitors while others draw fewer but higher-spending tourists. The purpose of visit also shifts the spending profile significantly.

🔍 Key insight: The ACT and NT appear below the trend line — they attract visitors but generate lower spend per night, suggesting shorter stays and a higher proportion of transit or business travellers.

6. Recovery has been uneven — and seasonality is back

Not all states have recovered at the same pace. Tourism patterns are also re-establishing their seasonal rhythms, with summer and school holidays driving peaks — a pattern that had largely vanished during the COVID years.
